# Competition digest — France-first motorcycle track-day product

## Scope and searches

- **Decision served:** identify an evidence-bounded product wedge, and what not to build, for a France-first motorcycle product connecting track sessions to maintenance by sessions/hours/cycles, season calendar and costs, proof documents, and a resale passport; cars remain second.
- **Competitive frame:** named products Apex Lines, MotoBook, MotoHist, MotoStack, LookOver, RideApp, TrackMate, and RaceChrono, plus non-consumption/manual substitutes.
- **Evidence firewall:** only web evidence retrieved in this run is admitted. No project files or prior knowledge are evidence.
- **Acquisition:** two rounds; 15 distinct admitted sources. Round 1 mapped official product/store pages. Round 2 followed pricing, update cadence, visible adoption, ownership, and current user commentary. Searches included exact product names plus `pricing`, `features`, `track day`, `maintenance`, `service history`, `App Store`, `Google Play`, `reviews`, and France-specific calendar/booking terms.
- **Freshness rule:** offer and pricing claims come from live pages or store listings crawled/accessed within three months. Older store-update dates are used only as trajectory/weakness evidence, not as proof that an old feature set is current.
- **Notation below:** **explicit** means the reviewed source affirmatively offers it; **partial** means an adjacent implementation; **not evidenced** is not a claim that the capability is absent.

**Admissibility conclusion (not a viability verdict):** no admitted product performs the whole job. Apex Lines and LookOver are the closest software substitutes; RideApp and TrackMate own the France event transaction; RaceChrono owns the mature telemetry transaction; MotoBook is the most relevant France-native maintenance substitute.

### What the combination shows

1. **The seam is still unoccupied in the admitted set.** France event platforms own booking/documents; track tools own sessions/telemetry; maintenance tools own intervals/receipts/resale. No reviewed offer explicitly turns a French booked event and its actual sessions into component wear, season actual-versus-forecast cost, and a buyer-verifiable component history.
2. **Apex Lines invalidates “track-linked maintenance is unique.”** It already links logged ride time to engine hours and tire heat cycles, includes readiness, and exports a resale-ready ledger. A defensible wedge therefore needs France-local workflow, costs and stronger proof/interoperability—not merely the linkage concept.
3. **LookOver invalidates “maintenance + documents + transfer + cars is unique.”** It combines usage-linked maintenance, costs, files, PDF/QR transfer and multi-vehicle support at a low freemium price. Track-session semantics and France event context must do the differentiating work.
4. **France calendar/booking is an occupied transaction.** RideApp has IPONE plus named organizer participation and current app releases; TrackMate exposes live inventory, payments, waivers, insurance and place resale. Building a third undifferentiated marketplace would compete for organizer supply and event checkout rather than exploit the cross-product seam.
5. **Non-consumption has zero switching cost and high flexibility.** Notes/Sheets/hour meters can be tailored to maintenance and expense tracking. The product must remove reconciliation/input work or create proof that the manual stack cannot, not merely offer cleaner forms.

## Evidence-bounded wedge hypotheses

These are inferences from the overlap map, not an overall viability verdict.

- **Primary wedge: the France track-season system of record, complementary to booking and timing tools.** Import or capture a RideApp/TrackMate booking and RaceChrono/RaceBox session evidence; convert session duration and tire use into user-auditable wear; reconcile event, travel, consumable and maintenance actuals against a season plan; keep organizer/insurance/receipt documents on the same event/component timeline.
- **Trust wedge: component-level proof, not a generic PDF.** Bind each service, receipt, session-hours increment and component move/retirement into a provenance trail the seller can share without exposing unrelated personal data. MotoBook, MotoHist, MotoStack and LookOver already claim certificates, stamps, PDFs or transfers, so “passport” alone is not differentiating; traceability from track event → session → component → work/receipt is the narrower gap.
- **Local moat candidate: France coverage and workflow adapters.** French circuits, organizer document types, insurance/waiver states, euros and season vocabulary are absent or partial in the global near-direct products. This only becomes defensible if maintained as structured coverage or integrations, not as static content.
- **Car second is consistent with the evidence.** RaceChrono and LookOver already span motorcycles and cars; competing generically across both from launch would erase the France-motorcycle specificity without adding a novel job.

## What not to build first

- **Do not build a standalone lap timer/telemetry analyzer.** RaceChrono is cheap, mature, deeply instrumented, actively updated and visibly adopted; use/import its data where possible. [S14]
- **Do not build an undifferentiated maintenance reminder or digital garage.** MotoBook is free in France; LookOver has aggressive freemium/lifetime pricing; MotoHist and MotoStack cover history/transfer. [S3–S9]
- **Do not build a third generic French track-day marketplace or organizer back office.** RideApp and TrackMate already compete for organizer inventory, checkout and event administration. [S10–S13]
- **Do not lead with “resale passport” as the whole proposition.** Four admitted products already use certification, stamps, QR transfer, buyer tokens or named Sale Passports. The differentiated claim must be stronger and demonstrable. [S3, S5, S7–S8]
- **Do not make racing lines, 3D circuit simulation, social community or generic AI service advice the MVP.** Those are occupied feature branches in Apex Lines, MotoHist and LookOver and do not close the evidenced cross-workflow gap. [S1, S5, S8]
- **Do not require double entry as the core experience.** The manual substitute is already flexible and free; value depends on automatic/session-assisted accrual, imports and reusable proof. [S15]

## Dead, weak and life signals

- **Apex Lines — alive, very early:** a release three days before access, but insufficient ratings, English only and no French circuits in its published list. Treat feature overlap as real and traction as unproven. [S1–S2]
- **MotoBook — visible installed base, weak release cadence:** Google Play shows 10K+ downloads, while its last listed update is February 2025. The live site remains active; this is a maintenance-risk signal, not evidence of death. [S3–S4]
- **MotoHist — alive, young and unverified:** legal owner incorporated September 2025; Premium remains “coming soon”; community, partner-garage and cryptographic-stamp scale has no independent confirmation in the admitted sources. [S5–S6]
- **MotoStack — live but very thin footprint:** current pricing/product page plus one Reddit mention; no app-store footprint, release log, usage count or independent review was found within budget. Traction is unknown. [S7, S15]
- **LookOver — alive, early:** July-audited feature page, May Android update and 1K+ downloads. It is a credible product threat despite modest visible adoption. [S8–S9]
- **RideApp — alive, early France traction:** launched February 2026, updated through July and had 18 French App Store ratings; an independent launch report names four organizer relationships. A review asking for more dates shows supply breadth is still a live weakness. [S10–S11]
- **TrackMate — alive inventory, opaque adoption:** current bookable calendar and organizer claims are visible, but no independent usage/review evidence was found; page counts conflict. [S12–S13]
- **RaceChrono — alive and established:** current July release, large review base and deep feature set; the developer's 100K-active claim remains unverified, but store-observed traction is still materially stronger than the early entrants. [S14]

## Contradictions and open questions

### Contradictions / uncertainty

- TrackMate's homepage showed **49 events / 14 partners**, while a current filtered calendar page showed **51 days / 20 circuits** and described **7 organizers**. Filters and live availability may explain some variance, but none of these counts should be treated as audited traction. [S12–S13]
- RideApp's promise of a unified date supply coexists with a current App Store review asking for more dates. This is not a capability contradiction; it is a supply-coverage warning. [S10–S11]
- MotoBook's live website implies ongoing availability while the Android binary's last published update is 18 months old. Web/PWA delivery could explain the difference; release cadence cannot be concluded from Android alone. [S3–S4]
- “Verified,” “certified,” “cryptographically signed,” and “resale-ready” are not equivalent. The reviewed sources do not establish what is independently verified, what is only timestamped/vendor-signed, or what legal/economic weight French buyers attach to it. [S3, S5, S7–S8]

### Open questions for a deepen pass

1. Can RideApp, TrackMate, RaceChrono or RaceBox export stable calendar/session/document data, or would integration depend on email/file imports and user consent?
2. Does MotoBook automatically accrue riding days/hours from activities, or only let the rider manually choose and update those units?
3. What French circuits and language support will Apex Lines add, and how quickly? Its current release velocity makes this wedge time-sensitive.
4. What are paid conversion, season retention, multi-bike incidence and French active-user counts for all early entrants? Public install/rating counts are insufficient.
5. What proof does a French used-track-bike buyer actually trust: original invoice, workshop signature, immutable timestamp, organizer/session evidence, component serial, or a transferable account record?
6. Is season cost forecasting a recurring decision users maintain, or a one-time spreadsheet exercise they abandon when totals become uncomfortable? The current evidence shows both explicit expense sheets and abandonment anecdotes, but not France-track prevalence.
7. Which minimum import path removes enough double entry to beat Notes/Sheets without first building a marketplace or telemetry engine?

## Round stopping reason

Stopped at the explicit two-round / 15-source cap. Coverage was sufficient to classify the eight named products and non-consumption, isolate the occupied product surfaces, and state wedge hypotheses. Traction, technical interoperability and buyer trust remain open because novelty did not exhaust before the source cap.
