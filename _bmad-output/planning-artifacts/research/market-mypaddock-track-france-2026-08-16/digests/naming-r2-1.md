# Naming and reputation reconnaissance: MyPaddock / MyPadock / Paddock

**Territory and market:** France / European Union; software for motorcycle and car track-day users  
**Accessed:** 2026-08-16  
**Method:** bounded web-only scan of official product sites, app stores, INPI DATA, EUIPO eSearch/TMview documentation, and indexed registry records  
**Evidence boundary:** no project files or internal project context were used

## Scope and caution

This is collision reconnaissance, not legal clearance, registrability advice, or a viability verdict. It distinguishes:

- **registered mark / application** only when a registry record is identified, with the source quality stated;
- **commercial product, company, app-store, or domain use**, which can create naming and reputation friction but is not proof of registered rights;
- **secondary registry lead**, which must be rechecked in the official register before relying on its status;
- **mere search observation**, including a result that did not surface in an index, which is never treated as proof that a mark or use does not exist.

The official INPI DATA, EUIPO eSearch and TMview search surfaces were opened. Their coverage and search methods were verified, but their JavaScript result lists did not render record-level results in this research environment. INPI's bulk API requires an account, and EUIPO's trademark-search API requires a subscribed, authenticated client. Consequently, this digest makes **no authoritative “zero hits” statement** for any candidate.

## Findings

### 1. The exact MyPaddock string already carries a prominent motorsport reputation

The exact **MyPaddock** name is in current, sustained use by Oracle Red Bull Racing for a global Formula 1 fan platform, with its own Red Bull Racing subdomain and explicit 2026 product commitments [N1–N2]. This creates a concrete search, recall, partnership and implied-affiliation collision to test independently of any unverified trademark position.

The earlier **The Paddock** identity matters too: Red Bull's own 2025 charter says that service launched in 2021, while a separate direct track-day app now trades as ThePaddock [N2, N4–N5]. Users may therefore encounter both the historic Red Bull usage and a current track-day product under closely related strings.

### 2. PaddockPro and ThePaddock are functional, not merely lexical, collisions

The direct overlap is unusually concrete:

- PaddockPro covers track-day setup, GPS timing, session data, analysis and community [N3].
- ThePaddock covers track days, lap logging, leaderboards, vehicle garages, community and event syncing, and is available through the French Apple storefront [N4–N5].

These are **commercial product uses**, not proven French/EU registrations. Nevertheless, their similar users, vocabulary and feature sets increase the operational likelihood of app-store confusion, misdirected searches, partner ambiguity and mistaken customer support contacts.

### 3. Bare Paddock is crowded across motorsport software

Paddock motorcycle tracking, PADDOCK social telemetry, Paddock.gg and Paddock Tracker all use the same dominant word for motorsport software [N6–N8]. Formula 1 separately claims Paddock Club variants in its current brand guidelines [N10]. An equine Paddock Pro product adds exact-name crowding in software outside motorsport [N9].

**Inference:** in this market, “paddock” behaves as a familiar category/setting word and is already heavily shared. A candidate dominated by that word will have to work harder for distinctiveness, search ownership and unambiguous word-of-mouth. This is a branding inference from the documented uses, not a legal conclusion about distinctiveness or enforceability.

### 4. The one-d spelling is a near variant, not an independent evidence base

No citable exact **MyPadock / My Padock** live product or official record surfaced in this bounded indexed-web scan. That is only a **negative search observation**; it is not evidence of availability.

Commercially, MyPadock remains one character away from MyPaddock and from the ordinary spelling “paddock.” It should be tested as a typo, autocorrection and pronunciation variant. The spelling change alone does not remove the documented Red Bull association or the broader Paddock naming cluster. This is a brand/search inference, not a trademark-similarity opinion.

### 5. The preliminary registry picture is incomplete by design

The official tools confirm that a proper search must cover exact, phrase, wildcard and fuzzy variants, relevant territories, all statuses and Nice classes [N11–N14]. The accessible secondary leads show:

- an exact historical PADDOCK EUTM reported expired, with narrow stock-yard/electric-fence goods [N15];
- a motorcycle-related DERBI-PADDOCK record whose current status is time-sensitive and unresolved here [N16];
- a newer PADDOCK SPORTS figurative/Class 35 record reported registered [N17].

None of those is an authoritative exact-hit result for **MyPaddock**, **MyPadock**, **PaddockPro** or **ThePaddock** in the intended software/services classes. Conversely, the fact that indexed searches did not surface such an official record is not evidence that none exists. A current official export is the unresolved dependency.

## Naming requirements implied by the evidence

Any name advanced beyond exploration should meet all of these requirements:

1. **Distinctive core:** include a memorable element that is not merely `My`, `The`, `Pro`, a hyphen, or the deletion of one `d` from Paddock. The distinctive element should dominate speech, search and app-store display.
2. **No implied affiliation:** avoid presentation that could plausibly suggest an Oracle Red Bull Racing, Formula 1 or Paddock Club relationship.
3. **Exact plus similarity search:** search all joined, spaced and misspelled variants together; do not evaluate `MyPadock` separately from `MyPaddock`.
4. **Goods/services fit:** inspect the actual wording, not just class numbers. A Class 9 electric-fence record is materially different from downloadable track-day software, while an app, telemetry or community specification could be close even when the name is not exact.
5. **Territorial fit:** cover INPI French marks, EUTMs, and WIPO international registrations designating France or the EU; include relevant EU national offices through TMview if rollout extends beyond France.
6. **Commercial discoverability:** check app stores, search results, domains and social handles in French and English. Record direct products even when no registered right is found.
7. **Expansion headroom:** the name must remain distinctive if the offer expands across motorcycle and car users, events, telemetry, community, marketplace and SaaS/operator tooling.
8. **Evidence capture before spend:** preserve dated official exports or screenshots showing owner, application/registration number, status, priority, territory, exact goods/services, opposition/cancellation history and renewal dates.

## Required official query matrix

Run and export these searches in both **DATA INPI** and **TMview/eSearch plus**:

- Exact/phrase: `MYPADDOCK`, `MY PADDOCK`, `MYPADOCK`, `MY PADOCK`, `PADDOCK`, `PADDOCKPRO`, `PADDOCK PRO`, `THEPADDOCK`, `THE PADDOCK`.
- Wildcard/contains: `*PADDOCK*`, `*PADOCK*`.
- Fuzzy spelling searches for `MYPADDOCK`, `MYPADOCK` and `PADDOCK`; commission INPI's orthographic, phonetic and conceptual similarity search if a candidate remains in contention.
- Territory/office: INPI/France, EUIPO/entire EU, and WIPO records designating France or the EU.
- Status: begin with **all** statuses; then separate Registered and Filed from Ended and Expired. Do not collapse an application into a registration.
- Provisional Nice scope to validate against the exact offer: **9 and 42** for downloadable software/apps and SaaS; **35, 38 and 41** where marketplace, advertising, communications/community, sport/event, timing, training or entertainment functions are included. Add **39 and/or 43** only if the product actually provides transport/travel or accommodation reservation services. Search the written goods/services in addition to the classes.
